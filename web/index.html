How it works

Four steps, and you can stop it any time you're alive

Your money waits in an address only the GhostProtocol contract controls, through NEAR Chain Signatures.

1

You check in

Now and then, with one tap. Missing a few check-ins alone moves nothing.

2

Witnesses confirm

Two of your three witnesses must sign that you have died. One is never enough.

3

A grace period runs

If you are alive, one check-in cancels everything. A witness can extend it if you are in hospital.

4

Your heirs get paid

The contract pays each heir between the minimum and maximum you set, and checks the payment landed.

Who can't change it

Every role has a limit written into the contract

The worst each one can do alone, tested on-chain.

The AI agent

Chooses an amount between your minimum and maximum, for a listed heir, once. If it goes silent, anyone can pay your heirs without it.

A thief with your phone

Can keep checking in, but all your witnesses together can still open a claim that only your offline recovery key can cancel.

Your witnesses

Can open a claim, but one check-in from you cancels it during the grace period.

A court

Can pause payment once, for a limited time set when the vault is made. It cannot stop it forever.

Whoever set it up

Holds no key to the vault once it is locked. Nobody does. The chain shows an empty key list.

A forged signature

Is thrown away. The contract checks every signature itself, and a payment counts only once the MPC network confirms it landed.

Questions

What people ask first

What if I'm in hospital and can't check in?

Missed check-ins alone do nothing. A claim needs your witnesses too, and any one of them can extend the grace period once to buy you time. One check-in from you cancels the claim.

What if someone steals my phone?

They can only check in, which delays a payout. All your witnesses together can open a claim that check-ins cannot cancel, and only your offline recovery key can stop it.

What if the AI is hacked?

The worst it can do is pick an amount between the minimum and maximum you set, for one of your listed heirs. It cannot add a recipient or pay twice. If it stops answering, a fallback pays each heir the maximum without it.

What if GhostProtocol disappears?

Nothing changes. The contract has no owner key, and anyone can trigger each step, including the payment, from any NEAR wallet.

Is this a legal will?

No. It is a program that pays out under fixed rules. It runs on testnet today and is a hackathon project, not a legal or financial product.
